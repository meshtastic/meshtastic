Every nRF52 device runs a small program called the bootloader before the firmware starts. In bootloader mode, also called DFU mode, the device mounts on your computer as a USB drive and installs whatever `.uf2` file is copied onto it. [Drag & Drop](/docs/getting-started/flashing-firmware/nrf52/drag-n-drop/), [Factory Erase](/docs/getting-started/flashing-firmware/nrf52/nrf52-erase/), and [Update nRF52 Bootloader](/docs/getting-started/flashing-firmware/nrf52/update-nrf52-bootloader/) all start here. The methods are ordered most convenient first.

## Reset button

Most nRF52 devices enter bootloader mode when you double-press the reset button within about half a second. Press the reset button once to leave it without flashing.

## Meshtastic client

Once Meshtastic firmware is installed, the device can be rebooted into bootloader mode from a client without touching a button. Connect the device over USB and run:

```shell
meshtastic --enter-dfu
```

The device reboots into bootloader mode and mounts the drive. The Android client's [USB File Transfer](/docs/software/android/user/firmware/#in-app-usb-update) option does the same over a USB connection.

## Devices with a single button

The SenseCAP Card Tracker T1000-E and SenseCAP MeshTracker X1 have one button and no reset button, so the double-press doesn't apply. On the [Meshtastic nRF52 Bootloader](https://github.com/meshtastic/nRF52_Bootloader) (formerly OTAFIX) 2.5 or later, connect the USB cable, then hold the button while the device starts and keep holding it for 3 seconds. The drive appears a few seconds later. A short press while the device starts, or a hold while the firmware is running, doesn't reach the bootloader. The steps are on each device page: [Card Tracker T1000-E](/docs/hardware/devices/seeed-studio/sensecap/card-tracker/#flashing) and [MeshTracker X1](/docs/hardware/devices/seeed-studio/sensecap/meshtracker-x1/#flashing).

To leave bootloader mode without flashing, eject the drive. Unplugging doesn't leave bootloader mode while firmware is installed: the device stays in it on battery, and plugging it back in only mounts the drive again.

A T1000-E on an nRF52 bootloader before OTAFIX 2.5 has no button path into the bootloader, and ejecting the drive doesn't leave it, so use the Meshtastic client method and copy a firmware file to get out.

## Devices with no firmware

A device with no valid firmware, for example after a bootloader package was flashed with `adafruit-nrfutil` or a firmware copy was interrupted, starts in bootloader mode by itself. What it does there depends on the [Meshtastic nRF52 Bootloader](https://github.com/meshtastic/nRF52_Bootloader) version:

- OTAFIX 2.1 to 2.5: it waits for a [Bluetooth OTA update](/docs/getting-started/flashing-firmware/nrf52/ota/) and shows no drive and no serial port. Use the reset button or the single-button hold to mount the drive instead.
- 2.6.0 and later: connected to a computer by USB, it mounts the drive on its own, with NO FIRMWARE as the title on boards with a display. On battery it waits for a Bluetooth OTA update, and connecting the USB cable while no phone is connected mounts the drive. Unplugging the cable from the drive returns it to the Bluetooth OTA wait. The bootloader gives a computer 3 seconds to recognize the drive and otherwise falls back to the Bluetooth OTA wait, so a charger or power bank is expected to behave like battery.

## Leaving bootloader mode by timeout

On 2.6.0 and later, a device with firmware installed that sits in bootloader mode for 6 minutes with no activity starts the firmware. A Bluetooth connection, a read or write on the drive, a USB connection and a serial update each restart the 6 minutes; a Bluetooth connection left idle does not. A device with no firmware keeps waiting.

## Checking your bootloader version

`INFO_UF2.TXT` on the mounted drive names the bootloader version, and from OTAFIX 2.4 on carries a `Factory-Erase:` line. On 2.6.0 and later it can also carry a `Last-Fault:` line, which records a bootloader fault for a bug report. [Update nRF52 Bootloader](/docs/getting-started/flashing-firmware/nrf52/update-nrf52-bootloader/#what-info_uf2txt-tells-you) explains each line and covers moving to a newer bootloader.
