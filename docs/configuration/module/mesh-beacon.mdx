## Overview

The Mesh Beacon Module lets a node periodically **advertise the existence of a mesh** to nodes that are not on it yet. A beacon carries a short human-readable message plus an optional "join offer": a channel, a region, and a modem preset.

This is the mechanism behind invitations like *"Join us on NarrowSlow"*. Because a beacon can be transmitted on radio settings other than your own, a node sitting on one preset and region can invite listeners who are on a different preset and region entirely - people who could not otherwise hear you at all.

A node can broadcast, listen, or both:

| Role            | Enabled by                 | What it does                                                                    |
| :-------------- | :------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Broadcaster** | `FLAG_BROADCAST_ENABLED`   | Periodically transmits beacons.                                                  |
| **Listener**    | `FLAG_LISTEN_ENABLED`      | Receives beacons and passes them to your client app, which shows the invitation. |

:::info
The module is deliberately **advisory**. The firmware never joins an advertised channel and never switches preset or region on its own. It hands the invitation to your client app, and nothing happens unless you accept it.
:::

Beacons are **zero-hop**: the mesh never rebroadcasts them, so only nodes in direct radio range hear yours. That is what keeps the feature from becoming a source of mesh-wide spam.

## Settings

The three on/off toggles are bits in a single `flags` value rather than separate switches. To change one, read the current value, set or clear the bit, and write the whole value back.

| Setting                   | Acceptable Values                                     | Default |
| :------------------------ | :---------------------------------------------------- | :------ |
| `broadcast_interval_secs` | `3600` or greater (`0` = default)                     | `3600`  |
| `broadcast_message`       | string, up to 100 bytes                               | `""`    |
| `broadcast_offer_channel` | channel settings (name, PSK, slot)                    | unset   |
| `broadcast_offer_preset`  | modem preset                                          | unset   |
| `broadcast_offer_region`  | region code                                           | `UNSET` |
| `broadcast_on_channel`    | channel settings (name, PSK, slot)                    | unset   |
| `broadcast_on_preset`     | modem preset                                          | unset   |
| `broadcast_on_region`     | region code                                           | `UNSET` |
| `broadcast_targets`       | up to 4 target entries                                | empty   |
| `flags`                   | `1` listen, `2` broadcast, `4` legacy split (OR them) | `0`     |

### broadcast_interval_secs

How often a beacon is transmitted. The floor is **3600 seconds (1 hour)**; `0` means "use the default", and any lower non-zero value is silently raised. The cadence survives a reboot: a node that restarts will not beacon again until a full interval has passed since its last real transmission.

### broadcast_message

The text carried in each broadcast, **capped at 100 bytes** and truncated if longer.

### broadcast_offer_channel, broadcast_offer_preset, broadcast_offer_region

The **join offer** - what your beacon advertises. Together these say *"there is a reachable mesh on this region and preset, here is the channel to use."* Any subset may be set: a message with no offer is valid, and so is an offer with no message.

An unknown region is cleared, and a preset that is not legal for that region is cleared.

:::warning
The advertised PSK is **not a secret**. It is a public join token, broadcast in the clear for anyone in range to read. If you want a genuinely private channel, share its PSK out of band and leave `broadcast_offer_channel` unset.
:::

### broadcast_on_channel, broadcast_on_preset, broadcast_on_region

The radio settings your beacon is **transmitted on**. Leave them unset and the beacon goes out on your node's current settings - a plain "message of the day" to whoever is already on your mesh. Set them and the radio is briefly switched to those settings for the transmission, then switched back, which is what lets you invite a mesh you are not on.

If the preset is not legal for the target region, both it and `broadcast_on_channel` are cleared.

:::warning
An unlicensed node will never transmit on a licensed-only (amateur) region. If you configure one, the beacon is dropped rather than sent - the firmware will not key up outside your licence.
:::

### broadcast_targets

A list of up to four destinations, for advertising to several meshes at once. When it is non-empty it replaces the single `broadcast_on_*` settings, and one beacon is sent per entry.

Each entry names a preset, a region, and a **channel slot from your own channel table** - so that channel has to exist on your node first, because your node needs its key to encrypt the beacon. Configure the channels, then write the beacon settings pointing at their slot numbers. Duplicate entries cost no extra airtime; they are skipped.

:::note
If an entry points at a slot you have not configured, nothing errors - the beacon simply goes out on your **current primary channel** instead, on that entry's preset and region. So a mistyped slot number silently advertises the wrong channel. Well-behaved client apps check this for you and warn.
:::

### flags

A single bitfield. OR the values together:

| Bit value | Name                     | Meaning                                                                                 |
| :-------- | :----------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `0`       | `FLAG_NONE`              | Nothing enabled.                                                                        |
| `1`       | `FLAG_LISTEN_ENABLED`    | Receive beacons. Without this, incoming beacons are dropped before your client sees them. |
| `2`       | `FLAG_BROADCAST_ENABLED` | Broadcast beacons from this node.                                                        |
| `4`       | `FLAG_LEGACY_SPLIT`      | Compatibility with older firmware. See below.                                             |

## Details

### One mesh or several, yours or someone else's

Two choices combine freely. **How many destinations:** one, using the `broadcast_on_*` settings, or several, using `broadcast_targets`. Neither is preferred and neither is legacy - pick whichever matches your deployment. **Whose radio settings:** leave them unset for your own current mesh, or set them to reach a different one.

So a single-target beacon with nothing overridden is a message of the day on your own mesh, and a target list can mix one entry on your current settings with others on different presets and regions.

### Legacy split

Setting this flag makes your beacons usable by nodes running firmware older than this module, in two ways:

- When a beacon has **both** text and an offer, it is sent as **two packets** - one carrying the offer, one an ordinary text message carrying the text - so that clients which do not understand beacons still display the text.
- Every beacon is stamped so that firmware older than 2.7.20 accepts it rather than discarding it. Beacons remain zero-hop.

:::note
A side effect of the second point: a legacy-split beacon appears to be **1 hop away** even though it arrived over direct radio. Do not read a beacon's hop count as a distance.
:::

### What happens when you receive one

With listening enabled, an incoming beacon is passed to your client app, which decides how to present it. The firmware caches the most recent offer but never acts on it, and deliberately does not turn a beacon's text into a normal message - a beacon is an announcement, not a message addressed to you, so it will not wake your device or appear in a conversation.

:::danger
Accepting an offered region or preset **changes your node's own radio and will drop it off its current mesh.** A good client app makes that consequence clear before you confirm.
:::

## Examples

A multi-region invitation beacon:

```text
flags                   = FLAG_BROADCAST_ENABLED | FLAG_LEGACY_SPLIT
broadcast_message       = "Join us on NarrowSlow!"
broadcast_offer_preset  = NARROW_SLOW
broadcast_offer_region  = EU_N_868
broadcast_offer_channel = { name: "MyChannel", psk: <32-byte key> }
broadcast_interval_secs = 3600
broadcast_targets = [
  { preset: LONG_FAST,   region: EU_868,   channel_index: 0 },
  { preset: NARROW_SLOW, region: EU_N_868, channel_index: 1 },
]
```

The same settings can be baked in at build time through `userPrefs.jsonc`, including per-target entries.
